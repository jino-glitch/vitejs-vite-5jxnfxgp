Find U.S. Wholesale Lavender Suppliers — Bunches, Sachets & Bulk Bud

OBJECTIVE
Find the best wholesale suppliers, growers, processors, importers and distributors of dried lavender currently selling in the United States. I want both finished goods and raw material.

PRODUCT SCOPE
A. Dried lavender bunches/bundles — decorative, 10-18" stems, wrapped or naked
B. Lavender sachets — filled fabric bags (muslin, organza, linen, cotton), retail-ready with hangtag
C. Bulk dried lavender bud — loose, by the pound, for sachet filling and blends
D. Adjacent: lavender essential oil, hydrosol, sachet fabric bags, packaging

CRITICAL LAVENDER-SPECIFIC VARIABLES — get these for every supplier
These determine price and quality far more than volume does:
- Species/variety: Lavandin (Grosso, Provence — high yield, camphor note, best for sachets and bunches) vs L. angustifolia / English (Royal Velvet, Hidcote, Munstead, Folgate — sweeter, pricier, culinary)
- Bud grade: #1 / #2 / #3, or "ultra" — grading conventions vary by supplier, so ask what theirs means
- Culinary vs craft grade, and whether food-safe certification exists
- Crop year and harvest date — lavender fades and loses scent with age; last season's bud is worth less
- Color retention and fragrance strength; whether bud is cleaned/de-stemmed and sifted
- Stem length, bunch stem count or weight for bunches
- Domestic-grown vs imported (most cheap U.S. bulk bud is imported from France, Bulgaria or China — establish which)

GEOGRAPHIC PRIORITY

4. Nationwide U.S.  5. U.S.-based online wholesalers shipping commercial quantities
Note: Sequim WA, the Pacific Northwest islands, and inland Southern California (Cherry Valley / Temecula area) are the known U.S. lavender-growing clusters — search those hard.
Exclude overseas exporters unless they already hold U.S. inventory, a U.S. warehouse or a real U.S. wholesale channel.

TWO STRATEGIES — research both
A. FINISHED: buy sachets and bunches ready to sell, or private-label them
B. RAW: buy bulk bud plus empty sachet bags and fill/pack ourselves; buy bunches loose and re-wrap
Determine which gives better margin. Note that lavender bud is dense and cheap to ship compared to bulky bunches — so the freight math differs by format. Say so explicitly.

Company · type (grower / processor / importer / wholesaler / contract packer) · city/state · farm or warehouse location · website · varieties grown or sold · grades offered · bunch and bud pricing · price per lb at 1 / 5 / 25 / 100 lb · MOQ · case pack · crop year · lead time · in-stock vs harvest-season-only · shipping terms · private label · custom sachet fabric/print · contact name · title · email · phone · LinkedIn · evidence they currently sell in the U.S. · source URLs

Prioritize reaching an actual person: Owner, Farm Manager, Sales Director, Wholesale Manager, or Private Label Manager.

HARD RULES
- Never fabricate a name, email, phone, price or MOQ. If not found, say "not found."
- Label every unverified figure as unverified.
- Verify the company is currently active — many lavender farms are seasonal, tiny, or closed. Check for recent activity, and flag any that look dormant.
- Lavender is seasonal: harvest is roughly June-August. Ask every supplier when their next crop is available and whether they're currently sold out.
- Retailers (Amazon, Etsy, Walmart) are for competitive research only. Trace upstream: retailer → wholesaler → processor → farm.

RESEARCH LOOP
Keep refining until results are mostly duplicates. Search U.S. lavender farm associations and festival exhibitor lists, floral and gift directories, Faire, FGmarket, trade shows, and publicly available import records.
