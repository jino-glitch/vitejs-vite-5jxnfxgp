Find U.S. Wholesale Lavender Suppliers — Bunches, Sachets & Bulk Bud

OBJECTIVE
Find the best wholesale growers, processors, importers and distributors of dried lavender currently selling in the United States. I want both finished goods and raw material.

PRODUCT SCOPE
A. Dried bunches — decorative, 10-18" stems, wrapped or naked
B. Sachets — filled fabric bags (muslin, organza, linen, cotton), retail-ready with hangtag
C. Bulk dried bud — loose, by the pound, for sachet filling and blends
D. Adjacent: essential oil, empty sachet bags, packaging

LAVENDER-SPECIFIC VARIABLES — get these for every supplier
These drive price and quality more than volume does:
- Species: Lavandin (Grosso, Provence — high yield, camphor note, best for sachets and bunches) vs L. angustifolia / English (Royal Velvet, Hidcote, Munstead — sweeter, pricier, culinary)
- Bud grade (#1/#2/#3 or "ultra") — conventions vary, so ask what theirs means
- Culinary vs craft grade; food-safe certification
- Crop year and harvest date — lavender fades and loses scent with age
- Color retention, fragrance strength, whether bud is cleaned, de-stemmed and sifted
- Stem length and bunch weight or stem count
- Domestic-grown vs imported — most cheap U.S. bulk bud comes from France, Bulgaria or China. Establish which.

GEOGRAPHY
1. Southern California  2. California  3. WA, OR, ID, MT, CO  4. Nationwide  5. U.S. online wholesalers shipping commercial quantities
Search the known U.S. growing clusters hard: Sequim WA, the Puget Sound islands, and inland Southern California (Cherry Valley / Temecula).
Exclude overseas exporters unless they hold U.S. inventory, a U.S. warehouse or a real U.S. wholesale channel.

TWO STRATEGIES — research both
A. FINISHED: buy sachets and bunches ready to sell, or private-label them
B. RAW: buy bulk bud plus empty bags and fill ourselves; buy bunches loose and re-wrap
Which gives better margin? Note bud is dense and cheap to ship while bunches are bulky and fragile — the freight math differs by format. Say so explicitly.

HARD RULES
- Never fabricate a name, email, phone, price or MOQ. If not found, write "not found."
- Label every unverified figure as unverified.
- Verify each company is currently active. Many lavender farms are tiny, seasonal or closed — flag anything dormant.
- Harvest runs roughly June-August. Ask every supplier when the next crop lands and whether they are currently sold out.
- Retailers (Amazon, Etsy, Walmart) are for competitive research only. Trace upstream: retailer → wholesaler → processor → farm.
- Keep refining searches until results are mostly duplicates. Cover lavender farm associations and festival exhibitor lists, floral and gift directories, Faire, FGmarket, trade shows, and public import records.
