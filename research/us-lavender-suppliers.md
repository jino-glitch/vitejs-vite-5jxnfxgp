## 1. Headline finding

**For lavender, the make-it-yourself math flips.** In the potpourri research I recommended buying finished, because potpourri is a bulky multi-ingredient product where freight and import-scale pod buying dominate. Lavender inverts every one of those factors:

| Factor | Potpourri | Lavender sachets |
|---|---|---|
| Density | Nearly all air — freight kills you | Bud is **dense** — cheap to ship |
| Ingredients | 15+ components, hero pods at import scale | **One ingredient** |
| Fragrance | Oil + fixative, IFRA compliance | **Self-fragrancing** — no oil needed |
| Assembly | Blend ratios, packaging orientation | Fill a bag, pull a drawstring |
| Local supply | Thin | **Bud, bags and farms all available in/near California** |

**Recommendation: Option B — buy bulk buds and empty muslin bags and fill sachets yourself.** Reasoning and cost skeleton in §7. Run Option A in parallel for the first 90 days to learn the market, and hold Option C (private label) as the scale play.

**Best single contact:** **Wayward Winds Lavender** (Newberg, OR) — the only U.S. lavender operation found with a fully structured four-tier wholesale program (bulk / ready-to-label / private label / their own label) including **in-house label design and printing**. Katie@waywardwindslavender.com.

**Best California finished-goods supplier:** **Sonoma Lavender Co.** (Santa Rosa) — established sachet brand, handmade in Sonoma County, sold through spas and fine retailers nationally.

**Closest to Southern California:** **123 Farm** (Cherry Valley, Riverside County) — but see the harvest-limit caveat in §4. **Lavender Fields Forever** (Buellton) is the better California farm bet.

### Grades — ask for these by name

**San Francisco Herb Co.** grades by colour, application and origin, and the distinction is directly actionable:

- **Super Blue** — blue-purple, better aroma. For potpourri, teas, bath herbs, culinary.
- **Extra Blue** — grey-purple, stronger and more piney. **"Perfect for sachets, pillows and bath herbs."**

**Extra Blue is your sachet grade** — and it is typically the cheaper of the two. Do not let a supplier sell you culinary-grade bud for a decorative sachet.

**Bulk Apothecary** uses the French commercial system: **Extra, Super, Surchoix**. **Starwest Botanicals** offers Extra and Super in both conventional and organic.

### Stem counts vary 5× — always ask

Cascade Floral ~50 stems/bunch · Mt Airy 100–125 · Norwood 125+ · McKinley ~250. A "bunch" is not a unit of measure. Quote comparisons are meaningless without stem count or weight.

## 7. Strategic comparison — A vs B vs C

### The honest cost skeleton for Option B

I will not invent prices. But the *structure* of the arithmetic is worth seeing, because it is what drives the recommendation. **Every number below is an assumption, clearly marked, that you must replace with a quote:**

- A 4"×6" muslin sachet holds roughly **0.5–1.0 oz** of bud → **16–32 sachets per pound** *(estimate)*
- At an assumed craft-grade bud cost of **$X/lb**, bud cost per sachet is **$X ÷ 16 to $X ÷ 32**
- Plain small muslin bags at volume are a commodity *(price not verified — get quotes from State Line and Gallant)*
- Labour is filling and pulling a drawstring — fast, unskilled. **Run a timed pilot** before modelling it.

The leverage is obvious: **one pound of bud becomes 16–32 retail units.** That ratio is why this works, and it is fundamentally different from potpourri, where a bag consumes most of a pound of expensive mixed material.

### The three models

| Dimension | **A — buy finished** | **B — buy buds + bags, fill ourselves** | **C — private label** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Highest. Mt Airy's $10/sachet at qty 12 is a farmers-market price. | **Lowest** — one dense ingredient plus a commodity bag | Middle |
| **MOQ** | Lowest. Purple Haze has **no minimums**; Mt Airy **12** | Moderate — bud by the 25–100 lb, bags by the case | Highest |
| **Labour** | Zero | Real but light and unskilled | Zero |
| **Freight** | Moderate | **Low** — bud is dense, bags are light and flat | Moderate |
| **Packaging control** | None | **Total** — your fabric, size, fill weight, tag | High |
| **Branding** | None — you resell their brand | **Total** | High |
| **Scalability** | Capped by farm harvests | **Excellent** — bud is a commodity from multiple suppliers | Good |
| **Lead time** | Days | Weeks (bag print is the long pole) | Months |
| **Margin** | Lowest | **Highest** | Middle |
| **Risk** | Lowest | Low — no fragrance formulation, no IFRA, no fixative, no blend ratios | Low |

### Recommendation

**Lead with Option B.** Unlike potpourri, there is no structural reason to pay someone else to assemble this product. There is one ingredient, it is self-fragrancing, it ships densely, and both the bud and the bags are available from suppliers inside California. The value added by a sachet manufacturer is a person putting buds in a bag — which is not worth the margin you'd surrender at any meaningful volume.

**Sequenced:**

1. **This month (harvest is landing).** Email **wholesale@purplehazelavender.com** for their price list — zero friction, no minimums. Simultaneously request bulk quotes from **Monterey Bay Herb Co.** and **San Francisco Herb Co.**, specifying **Extra Blue / craft grade, 2026 crop**.
2. **Same week.** Call **State Line Bag** and **Gallant International** for muslin bag quotes at 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000, plain and custom-printed.
3. **Sampling.** Order Mt Airy's 12-sachet minimum and a Sonoma Lavender sachet. Weigh the fill, measure the bag, assess bud colour and scent strength. That gives you the physical spec to quote against.
4. **Pilot.** Fill 200 units by hand and time it. That single number decides whether B beats C at your volume.
5. **Hold Option C in reserve.** If the pilot shows labour is worse than expected, **Wayward Winds** is ready-made for it — ready-to-label product with in-house printing means you can switch to private label without redesigning anything.
6. **Option A stays useful** for bunches. Bunches are fragile, seasonal and awkward to assemble — buying those finished from **Mt Airy at $6.00 delivered** or **Lavender Fields Forever** is sensible even while you self-fill sachets.

**Split the strategy by format: fill your own sachets, buy your bunches finished.** That is the highest-margin configuration available.
